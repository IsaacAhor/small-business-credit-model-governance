# Adverse-Action Reason Review

This review path focuses on whether a credit union can document and review
specific reasons for adverse credit decisions when an AI-enabled third-party
tool supports small-business or member-business underwriting.

This is a governance and documentation protocol. It is not legal advice. A
credit union should have counsel or qualified compliance staff confirm the
applicable notice duties for the product, applicant type, gross-revenue band,
delivery channel, and facts of the decision.

## Business-Credit Scoping

Before reviewing reason quality, identify the business-credit category.

| Category | Review focus |
| --- | --- |
| Business credit for businesses with gross revenues of $1 million or less, or to start a new business | Confirm the applicable Regulation B notice path, timing, delivery method, and specific-reason support before relying on a workflow. |
| Business credit for businesses with gross revenues over $1 million | Confirm the applicable notification, written-reason request, and retention path rather than assuming consumer-credit treatment. |
| Trade credit, factoring, or similar credit | Confirm the applicable path with counsel before applying this protocol. |
| Mixed consumer and business products | Confirm whether consumer-credit notice controls also apply. |

The NCUA Regulation B guide includes checklist items for small-business and
larger-business credit. This run kit uses those public checklist themes as
review prompts, not as a substitute for applying the regulation to a specific
credit union.

## Applicability, Retention, And Related-Notice Review

For the identified path, the responsible compliance or legal reviewer should
record the applicability basis, notice timing and delivery treatment, specific-
reason treatment, and records-retention treatment. The record should also flag
whether consumer-report, risk-based-pricing, electronic-disclosure, or
Section 1071 data, firewall, and recordkeeping obligations may be implicated.
Those topics have separate conditions and must not be assumed from the use of
an AI vendor alone.

## Minimum Reason Evidence

For every declined, counteroffered, or otherwise covered adverse decision, the
review record should preserve:

- application or decision ID
- action taken
- product and business-credit category
- model or rule version
- threshold-set version
- reason-code mapping version
- ranked decision drivers or rule triggers
- generated reasons or notice text
- notice version, delivery channel, and notice-date evidence where applicable
- whether reasons were generated by vendor, credit union, or combined workflow
- manual overrides or policy overlays
- retention schedule and retrieval owner
- any consumer-report, electronic-record, or Section 1071 applicability flag
- review timestamp
- reviewer notes

## Reason Accuracy Tests

| Test | Review question | Failure example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Completeness | Does every covered adverse decision have a reason record or right-to-reasons path? | Declined decision without any reason record. |
| Traceability | Can each reason be traced to a model driver, policy rule, or documented decision factor? | Reason text appears in the notice but no driver or rule supports it. |
| Specificity | Does the reason identify the principal basis for the decision rather than a generic outcome? | "Did not meet internal standards" with no specific factor. |
| Mapping integrity | Is the reason code valid under the mapping version active at decision time? | Reason code was retired before the decision date. |
| Rank discipline | Are the top reasons aligned to the largest or controlling adverse drivers? | Low-impact factor is listed while a stronger adverse driver is omitted. |
| Change stability | After model or mapping changes, did reason behavior change in a documented way? | New model version changes reason mix without change review. |
| Human override clarity | If staff overrode the AI output, is the actual reason for action preserved? | Notice reasons mirror the model even though a manual policy exception controlled. |
| Retention | Are reasons, inputs, mappings, and notices retained for the required review period? | Reason output is overwritten after a vendor refresh. |

## Vendor Questions

Ask the AI vendor:

- Can you produce ranked reason drivers for each adverse decision?
- Are reasons based on the model, rules, policy overlays, or post-processing?
- How do you prevent generic, misleading, stale, or unmapped reason codes?
- How do you test reason accuracy?
- What happens when model features are not understandable to applicants or
  reviewers?
- Can the credit union configure or approve reason mappings?
- How are reason mappings versioned?
- What evidence is retained after a decision, how can it be retrieved, and what
  contract terms protect its availability after a vendor change or exit?
- How are manual reviews, overrides, and exceptions reflected in reasons?
- Can the vendor support the credit union's separately determined notice,
  retention, consumer-report, electronic-disclosure, and Section 1071 paths?
- What support will you provide during compliance, complaint, audit, or examiner
  review?

## Practical Review Output

A completed adverse-action review should include:

- scope and product category
- applicable business-credit pathway to be checked by counsel or compliance
- recorded applicability, notice, and retention basis
- vendor reason-generation description
- reason-code mapping reviewed
- sample decisions reviewed
- QA failures and remediation items
- model-change or mapping-change findings
- unresolved limitations
- decision on whether the reason workflow is acceptable, conditional, or not
  ready for use
- reviewer signoff

## Escalation Triggers

Escalate to compliance, counsel, model-risk, or vendor-management owners when:

- declined decisions lack reason records
- reason codes cannot be traced to decision drivers
- the vendor cannot explain how reasons are produced
- reasons change materially after model updates without documented review
- notice text is generic or inconsistent with the actual decision factor
- manual overrides are not reflected in the reason record
- the vendor refuses to support audit, complaint, or examiner review
- retention, mapping version, or data lineage evidence is missing
