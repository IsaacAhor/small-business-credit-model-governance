# Source Map

This file maps public source themes to the controls in the credit-union AI
vendor-risk run kit. The mapping is informational and is not legal advice.

## NCUA Artificial Intelligence Resources

Source:
<https://ncua.gov/regulation-supervision/regulatory-compliance-resources/artificial-intelligence-ai>

| NCUA theme | Run-kit coverage |
| --- | --- |
| Credit unions may use AI when implemented safely, soundly, and compliantly. | Scope definition, business-fit review, risk assessment, board and management oversight, and limitations. |
| NCUA has not issued AI-specific rules; existing technology-neutral requirements still apply. | Limitations section and legal-review checkpoints that avoid treating this profile as a new rule or compliance certification. |
| Examiners evaluate safety and soundness, applicable compliance, internal controls, ongoing monitoring, and third-party due diligence. | Due-diligence questionnaire, monitoring protocol, issue register, reviewer signoff, and board/management reporting summary. |
| Credit unions using AI should identify unique AI risks, monitor and measure those risks regularly, and implement controls for operational, compliance, and security risks. | AI risk taxonomy, monitoring metrics, thresholds, breach handling, model-change review, and data-security controls. |
| Credit unions using AI vendors should understand product function, introduced risks, business-model fit, and vendor safeguards, reliability, and controls. | Vendor function inventory, model transparency review, safeguards checklist, reliability review, service-level reporting, and contract controls. |
| NCUA encourages feedback through AskNCUA where clarity would support responsible AI adoption. | The run kit can be packaged as a practical example or question set for feedback, but only after limitations are preserved and no NCUA approval is implied. |

## NCUA Third-Party Relationship Guidance

Sources:
<https://ncua.gov/regulation-supervision/letters-credit-unions-other-guidance/evaluating-third-party-relationships-0>

<https://ncua.gov/regulation-supervision/letters-credit-unions-other-guidance/due-diligence-over-third-party-service-providers>

| NCUA theme | Run-kit coverage |
| --- | --- |
| Credit unions should conduct risk assessment and due diligence before entering third-party arrangements. | Pre-adoption risk assessment and vendor due-diligence questionnaire. |
| Outsourcing does not remove the credit union's responsibility for safety and soundness. | Oversight ownership, board/management reporting, controls, and limitations. |
| Credit unions need infrastructure, staff, and knowledge to monitor third-party arrangements. | Staff expertise, role assignment, monitoring cadence, and escalation ownership. |
| Officials should understand the third party's business model, financial condition, legal issues, contract rights, and service expectations. | Business-model review, financial/operational review, legal-review checklist, and service-level monitoring. |
| After adoption, credit unions should implement ongoing controls, monitoring, reporting, and quality control. | Monitoring protocol, issue register, periodic reporting, breach handling, and reviewer signoff. |

## NCUA Compliance Management and Regulation B Resources

Sources:
<https://ncua.gov/regulation-supervision/manuals-guides/federal-consumer-financial-protection-guide/compliance-management/compliance-management-systems-and-compliance-risk>

<https://ncua.gov/regulation-supervision/manuals-guides/federal-consumer-financial-protection-guide/compliance-management/lending-regulations/equal-credit-opportunity-act-regulation-b>

| NCUA theme | Run-kit coverage |
| --- | --- |
| A compliance management system includes board and senior management oversight, policies and procedures, training, monitoring and corrective action, member complaint response, and compliance audit. | Governance ownership, policy mapping, training questions, complaint monitoring, corrective-action tracking, and audit-ready documentation. |
| ECOA and Regulation B apply to credit unions, with NCUA enforcement authority for federally chartered credit unions under its supervision with $10 billion or less in assets. | Adverse-action reason review and legal-review checkpoints. |
| NCUA's Regulation B checklist includes small-business and larger-business credit adverse-action questions. | Separate business-credit review prompts for businesses with gross revenues of $1 million or less, startup businesses, and larger-business credit. |
| The fair-lending procedures treat statistical analysis as a scoping input and require factual review before conclusions. | Fair-lending screens are framed as governance review triggers, not legal conclusions. |

## NCUA CUSO Guidance

Source:
<https://ncua.gov/regulation-supervision/letters-credit-unions-other-guidance/expansion-permissible-cuso-activities-and-associated-risks/guidance-statement>

| NCUA theme | Run-kit coverage |
| --- | --- |
| Credit unions using CUSOs or third parties for lending should conduct due diligence and review compliance risk. | CUSO/fintech relationship inventory, contract review, and compliance evidence requests. |
| Third parties often use AI and ML to make lending decisions. | Explicit third-party AI underwriting scope. |
| Prudent credit unions understand models and risks, explain how models work, evaluate algorithm/data bias risk, and collaborate with model-risk experts. | Model transparency review, adverse-action reason review, fair-lending risk screening, and independent review prompts. |
| CUSO relationships can raise UDAAP, operational, privacy, and member-confusion risks. | Marketing/role clarity, privacy/data controls, complaint monitoring, and member-facing responsibility review. |

| Source theme | Run-kit coverage |

| AI data security includes provenance, integrity, access control, encryption, secure storage, and drift monitoring. | Data-source, data-lineage, data-integrity, API, access-control, encryption, and drift-monitoring questions. |
